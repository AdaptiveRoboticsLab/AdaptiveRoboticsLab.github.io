## Soft Robots

Soft robots have deformable bodies and muscle-like actuators, which makes them fundamentally different from traditional robots with rigid links and motor-based actuators. We work on:

- New actuation methods for soft robots, especially twisted-and-coiled actuators (TCAs)
- Fast, strong motion with bistable and multistable mechanisms
- Shape morphing: moving to and holding different configurations

## Flying Robots

Aerial robots suffer from limited airborne time due to low aerodynamic efficiency and high energy use. Perching on objects such as walls, power lines, or ceilings lets them save or even harvest energy while holding a desired altitude and orientation for surveillance or monitoring.

We enable perching for palm-size flying robots by combining computational and mechanical intelligence: onboard vision to estimate parameters such as time-to-contact, and compliant legs and grippers built from bistable mechanisms to attach to targets.

## Reconfigurable Robots

Most robots use each body part for one function, fixed by their mechanical design. We investigate how to embed variable-stiffness materials and reconfigurable modules (e.g., origami-inspired and tensegrity structures) into mechanisms, so the same structure can change configuration and serve different functions, such as walking, crawling, grasping, or rolling with shape-changing wheels.
