Adaptive Robotics Lab

Adaptive Robotics Lab

Department of Mechanical Engineering · Colorado State University

We design and build novel, small, and adaptive robots that can reconfigure their shapes, structures, or functionalities to fulfill tasks in diverse environments.

Our research Publications

The Adaptive Robotics Laboratory is founded and directed by Professor Jianguo Zhao and is located at the Powerhouse Energy Campus in Fort Collins, Colorado. We explore the fundamental science and applications of adaptive robots: robots that actively adapt to different environments and tasks by changing their shapes, sizes, motions, and functions. Our approach integrates reconfigurable mechanisms, variable-stiffness materials, biologically inspired principles, and advanced control.

Applications span robotics-inspired biology (how robots can help unravel principles of animal locomotion), biomedical devices (e.g., devices that assist the human heart), and environmental monitoring and inspection.

Research areas

Soft Robots

Artificial muscles, bistable mechanisms for fast and strong motion, and shape morphing.

Flying Robots

Perching for palm-size aerial robots using onboard vision and compliant bistable grippers.

Reconfigurable Robots

Variable-stiffness joints, origami and tensegrity modules that let one body serve many functions.
